1. **Indexes.** A unique index on employees.emp_code prevents duplicate employee codes. A unique compound index on attendance_logs(emp_code, date) prevents duplicate records for an employee/date and supports that lookup. Date-based indexes help attendance and late queries; (department, joined_on) supports headcount filtering. I would reject a separate attendance emp_code index because the compound index already starts with that field.
2. **Punch-in race.** An existence check alone cannot prevent simultaneous requests from racing. The unique (emp_code, date) index is the safeguard: one insert succeeds, and the other hits a duplicate-key error translated to 409 Conflict.
3. **Ties.** The leaderboard uses competition ranking: equal totals share a rank and the next rank is skipped, for example 1, 2, 2, 4. Applying the limit after ranking means a tie at the cutoff can return more results than requested. Employee code gives stable ordering.
4. **Headcount.** The department summary starts from eligible employees and combines attendance afterward. Employees who joined by month-end count even when they have no logs.
5. **One thing you would change** I would benchmark realistic data, inspect explain statistics, and tune indexes to actual filters and sorting before considering larger architecture changes.
